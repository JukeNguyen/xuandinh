# Asset Production Guide
### Photography & Video Production Bible

**Relationship to prior documents:** expands [`BLUEPRINT_V2_ADDENDUM.md`](./BLUEPRINT_V2_ADDENDUM.md) §3 (Visual Language), §4 (Art Direction), and §8 (Asset Production Guide) into a standalone, production-ready checklist. V2 remains the source of the *aesthetic rules*; this document is the *shot-by-shot execution plan* a photographer, videographer, or stock-sourcing team can work from independently.

**How to use this document:** each category below is a shot list with purpose, lighting mood, and lens guidance. Nothing here is optional flavor — every shot exists because a specific section of the page (cross-referenced) needs it. If a shoot day runs short on time, cut by category priority (Hero and Founder are non-negotiable; Travel and Drone are the first to cut if budget/time is constrained).

## Video Specifics

### Drone
Establishing/landscape-scale shots only — used sparingly for scale, never for spectacle. Best paired with Outdoor category locations. Cut first under budget constraints (per V2 §8 priority note).

### Close-up
Reserved for the highest-tension emotional beats: eyes, hands, breath, water/sweat detail. Used specifically in Problem/Agitation-adjacent video content and the VSL's tension beats — overusing close-ups elsewhere dilutes their impact.

### Talking Head
Single hard key light, minimal set (concrete or dark neutral backdrop, matching Background Treatments below). Founder and testimonial subjects both use this identical setup for visual consistency across the whole video library. Camera at or slightly above eye level — never a low angle (reads as either overly dramatic or unintentionally unflattering).

### B-roll
Tactile, detail-oriented: hands wrapping, boots lacing, water on face, breath in cold air, chalk dust, equipment texture. This is what carries the "controlled intensity" tone better than wide action shots — prioritize accordingly if time is short.

### Transitions
Hard cuts or simple cross-fades only, in both edited video and on-page section transitions. No whip-pans, spin transitions, or light-leak transitions — these read as social-media/influencer content and undercut the cinematic-restraint positioning (V2 §4).

## Sound Design & Music Style

**Music:** sourced from a premium cinematic library (Epidemic Sound / Artlist tier — not generic royalty-free stock). Two moods, mapped to narrative beats:
- **Tension** — low-BPM, minimal, used under Problem/Agitation and VSL setup.
- **Restrained rising hybrid-orchestral** — used under Curriculum/Transformation and results moments; "restrained" is load-bearing here — avoid anything that sounds like a movie-trailer cliché.

Explicitly avoid: generic "corporate uplifting" stock music — an immediate tell of low production value in this genre.

**Sound design:** subtle whoosh on video-internal transitions only. Breathing/heartbeat SFX reserved for VSL tension beats exclusively. All page-level UI sound is **off by default**, full stop — no exceptions, this is an accessibility and UX baseline, not a creative preference (V2 §6).

## Technical Specs

### Lighting
Low-key, single hard directional source as the default setup across nearly every category above. Two governing moods, applied deliberately per narrative beat, not randomly per shoot day:
- **Cold blue, pre-dawn** — before/struggle/discipline-in-progress imagery.
- **Warm gold rim** — after/transformation/achievement imagery.
Every shoot day should be scheduled and shot with this mapping decided in advance — it is a continuity requirement, not a per-shot improvisation.

### Lens Recommendations
- **24mm** — wide environmental/establishing shots, scale-emphasis compositions.
- **35mm** — general environmental portraits, most lifestyle/community shots.
- **50mm** — the default workhorse lens for most portrait and mid-shot work — natural perspective, minimal distortion.
- **85mm** — tight emotional close-ups (eyes, hands, breath) — shallow depth of field, isolates the subject from environment.

### Color Grading
Desaturated shadows, crushed (not muddy) blacks, warm highlight rim confined to skin tones and gold accents. Subtle teal-orange split toning — restrained and filmic (reference: Kodak 2383 print stock look), explicitly not a blockbuster-trailer-cliché grade. One consistent LUT/grading recipe applied across all photography and video so the entire asset library reads as one continuous visual world, regardless of shoot day or location.

## Styling

### Wardrobe
Neutral/muted tones only: black, charcoal, olive, navy, stone. No visible logos or graphics on clothing — avoids dating the content and avoids inadvertently advertising a different brand (blur or avoid visible third-party athletic-brand logos for both licensing and brand-neutrality reasons). Natural fabric textures (cotton, wool, technical training fabric). No bright colors. White is used only for a single deliberate "clean/discipline" moment (e.g., a plain white t-shirt in a controlled portrait) — not as a general wardrobe default.

### Props
Minimal, and every prop should carry real narrative meaning, not set-dressing filler: a plain notebook/journal, a stopwatch or plain analog clock, a barbell/kettlebell, a cold-plunge tub or ice, an unbranded water bottle. Avoid prop clutter entirely — an overstyled frame contradicts the "restraint is premium" design principle.

### Backgrounds
Concrete/industrial interiors, natural landscapes at dawn/dusk, minimalist home/training spaces. Explicitly avoid: busy environments with visible third-party branding or signage, cluttered commercial gyms, and obvious green-screen/studio-cyc backdrops (reads as corporate stock photography, directly on the never-use list).

---

## Editing Pace

Slow-to-medium baseline for brand film and VSL content — target average shot length of 3–5 seconds, letting shots breathe rather than cutting quickly. Reserve a faster pace (1–2 second average shot length) specifically for "results montage" hype moments — the deliberate pace *contrast* between calm baseline and fast montage is itself what signals "this part is different," so the baseline pace must stay genuinely slow for the contrast to register.

## Delivery Specs

### Aspect Ratios
- **16:9** — primary website video (hero background loop if adopted, VSL embed).
- **9:16** — social/Reels/Shorts, and a candidate mobile hero crop.
- **1:1** — square social feed placements.
- **4:5** — Instagram feed-optimal crop.

**Capture guidance:** shoot wide/raw at the highest practical resolution and recompose down to each ratio in post, rather than shooting natively in multiple aspect ratios on set — this preserves flexibility and resolution headroom. Exception: talking-head content intended primarily for social distribution is worth capturing natively in 9:16 for authenticity, since a re-composed landscape talking-head often reads as visibly repurposed.

### Social Crops
Landscape (16:9) footage requires a genuine vertical-safe recompose pass for any 9:16 export — never a blind center-crop, since the subject can fall outside the frame depending on original composition. Maintain a safe margin free of essential subject/text on all four edges for every vertical crop, anticipating platform UI overlays (captions, buttons) that vary by platform.

### Thumbnail Rules
High-contrast, single-subject framing consistent with the rest of the visual language (§Color Grading). If text-on-thumbnail is used at all, it follows the brand's typographic system (Design System §Typography) with the gold accent reserved for a single emphasized word, not the whole headline.

**Explicitly avoid:** shocked-face/exaggerated-expression thumbnails, red circles/arrows, or any of the loud clickbait conventions standard in fitness/self-improvement YouTube content. This is a deliberate differentiation choice, not an oversight — the brand's "quiet confidence" personality (V2 §2) should extend even to thumbnail design, where nearly every competitor in this space defaults to loud, urgent-looking thumbnails. A calm, high-contrast, restrained thumbnail will look different from the rest of the category by design.
